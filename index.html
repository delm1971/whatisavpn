What is a VPN?

A virtual private network (VPN) is a service that creates an encrypted connection between your device and a VPN server. Websites generally see the VPN server’s public IP address instead of your home IP address.

How does a VPN work?

When you connect to a VPN, your device sends traffic through an encrypted tunnel to a VPN server. The server then connects to websites on your behalf. This can help protect data in transit, especially on untrusted networks.

What can a VPN protect?

A VPN can help reduce exposure of your browsing traffic to local network observers and conceal your home IP address from sites you visit. HTTPS still matters, and VPN providers can potentially see some connection metadata.

What a VPN cannot do

A VPN does not make you anonymous, stop every phishing attempt, remove malware already on your device, or prevent websites from identifying you when you sign in. It also cannot guarantee access to every streaming service.

When should you use a VPN?

Consider a VPN when using unfamiliar Wi-Fi, traveling, or wanting to limit your internet provider’s visibility into your traffic destinations. Choose a reputable provider and keep your devices updated.
